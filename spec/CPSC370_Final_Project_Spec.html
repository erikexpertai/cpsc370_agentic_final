Project Overview

In this project, each team will design an LLM-based scheduling agent for one of Chapman University's 11 schools and colleges. Your agent will receive a set of available meeting times and classroom allocations from an instructor-provided university scheduling coordinator, the provost orchestrator. Using those resources, your agent must create a feasible schedule for its academic unit, explain its choices, respond to proposed schedule changes, and repair its schedule when resources or constraints change.

All 11 academic-unit agents will be integrated at the end of the semester with a common coordinator. The final system will therefore be a class-built multi-agent scheduling environment: each unit controls its own local scheduling decisions while the coordinator manages university-wide conflicts and requests changes.

The project is intentionally designed so that strong programming is not required. You may use any contemporary LLM agent framework, a simple Python wrapper, prompt/configuration files, retrieval over historical schedule data, or a combination of approaches. What matters is whether your agent behaves correctly, uses evidence and constraints appropriately, and conforms exactly to the common interface described in this specification.

What the Coordinator Provides Updated

Your agent does not choose the university-wide room inventory or time grid. The coordinator provides the resource envelope your academic unit may use:

  • A list of courses/sections that your academic unit must schedule.
  • A list of available meeting times (a canonical university grid of about 29 slots).
  • A list of classrooms allocated or available to your unit, including shared rooms that other units also hold.
  • Room capacity and relevant room features.
  • Current faculty/course constraints supplied for the scheduling run.
  • Any university-wide hard constraints that apply to all agents.

The full envelope is included in every request (generate_schedule, evaluate_change and repair_schedule), so your agent can be stateless. The envelope can change during a run: disruptions and coordinator reservations are written into it. Always use the envelope in the current request, never a cached copy.

Where conflicts come from. About 44% of Chapman classrooms were historically used by more than one school, some instructors teach in more than one unit, and some courses are cross-listed between units. The coordinator gives shared rooms to every unit that uses them, so units will collide. Resolving those collisions is the multi-agent part of the project.

What Your Agent Decides

  • Which course is assigned to which available time.
  • Which allocated classroom is assigned to each course.
  • How to resolve competing preferences within your unit.
  • Which proposed schedule changes are acceptable.
  • Which alternatives to suggest when a proposed change is unacceptable.
  • How to repair the unit schedule when a room, time, faculty availability, or other resource changes.

Use of Historical Schedule Data Updated

You may use historical Chapman schedule data in any way you believe improves your agent. Historical data is optional and advisory; it must never override current hard constraints.

  • Use prior schedules directly as examples in the agent prompt.
  • Retrieve similar historical courses or schedules at runtime.
  • Compute empirical preferences, such as common meeting days or times for a course or faculty member.
  • Infer common room types or scheduling patterns.
  • Use historical schedules to construct few-shot examples.
  • Ignore historical data entirely and rely on explicit current constraints.

If you use historical data, document exactly how it is used and identify any limitations. Copying a previous semester's schedule without reasoning about the current resource allocation is not sufficient.

Historical evidence in the provost's data

The full registrar schedule is not distributed. Historical practice reaches your agent through the envelope:

  • Each course's preferred_times is the slot its section historically met in, snapped to the current grid.
  • Each instructor's preferred_times collects the slots they historically taught in.
  • Room features are inferred from what historically met in each room.

Instructors appear only as pseudonyms (FAC-xxxxxx), and the same pseudonym is used in practice and on integration day. If you want more history, you can build your own: record the requests from practice runs with --save-dir, or use public sources you are authorized to use.

Scheduling Rules Updated

Your agent must distinguish between hard constraints and soft preferences. Hard constraints determine whether a schedule is valid. Soft preferences determine whether one valid schedule is better than another.

Hard constraints

  • A course must use one of the time slots supplied by the coordinator.
  • A course must use one of the rooms supplied or allocated to the unit.
  • Two courses may not occupy the same room at the same time.
  • One instructor may not teach two courses at the same time.
  • Expected enrollment may not exceed room capacity.
  • A course requiring a specialized room feature must be assigned to a room with that feature.
  • Explicit course-pair conflict constraints supplied in the input must be respected.
  • Any additional hard constraints included in the request must be respected. In Protocol 1.0 these are:
    • room unavailable_times
    • faculty unavailable_times
    • blackout windows
    • forbidden_timeslots
    These are described under Protocol Details below.
"At the same time" means overlapping, not the same ID. Two timeslots conflict if they share at least one day and their clock intervals overlap, even when their timeslot_ids differ. For example, MWF-1000 (10:00–10:50) overlaps MW-1000 (10:00–11:15). Day codes are M T W R F, where R is Thursday.
A response containing an avoidable hard-constraint violation is considered invalid, even if the schedule is otherwise attractive. The provost re-checks every schedule you return. Entries that violate a hard constraint are dropped, and those courses count as unresolved.

Soft preferences

Soft preferences may differ by academic unit and may be informed by historical schedules. Examples include:

  • Faculty preferred teaching days or times.
  • Avoiding early morning, late evening, or Friday sections.
  • Keeping required courses from the same program separated.
  • Minimizing long gaps in a faculty member's teaching day.
  • Using historically common meeting patterns.
  • Keeping a course in a preferred building or room type.
  • Minimizing changes when repairing an existing schedule.
  • Preferring rooms marked "shared": false, which other units can't contest.

Your team decides how to represent and prioritize soft preferences. You may use a weighted scoring function, LLM reasoning, rules, an optimizer, or a hybrid approach. You must be able to explain your choice.

Every academic-unit agent must support all three operations below.

Technical Freedom

You may implement the internals of your agent however you want, provided it follows the required interface. Examples include:

  • A simple Python program that calls an LLM.
  • OpenAI Agents SDK, LangGraph, CrewAI, PydanticAI, Google ADK, Microsoft Agent Framework, or another agent framework.
  • A prompt-driven agent with configuration files.
  • Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) over historical schedules or policy documents.
  • A hybrid system combining deterministic constraint checks with LLM reasoning.
  • A mathematical optimizer used as a tool by an LLM agent.

Using a sophisticated framework does not automatically earn a higher grade. A simple system that is reliable, explainable, and well evaluated may outperform a complex system.

Common request envelope

{
  "protocol_version": "1.0",
  "request_id": "req-001",
  "operation": "generate_schedule",
  "unit": "Fowler School of Engineering",
  "semester": "Fall 2027",
  "payload": { ... }
}

Required fields:

  • protocol_version: must be "1.0".
  • request_id: unique identifier supplied by the coordinator; return it unchanged.
  • operation: one of generate_schedule, evaluate_change, or repair_schedule.
  • unit: the academic unit your agent represents (full name, as in the table above).
  • semester: scheduling context.
  • payload: operation-specific input described below.

Common response envelope

{
  "protocol_version": "1.0",
  "request_id": "req-001",
  "unit": "Fowler School of Engineering",
  "status": "completed",
  "result": { ... },
  "warnings": [],
  "errors": []
}
  • status must be completed, partial, or failed.
  • result contains operation-specific output.
  • warnings contains non-fatal issues or uncertainty.
  • errors contains fatal problems that prevented completion. It must be non-empty when status is failed.

Operation 1: generate_schedule

Request payload (abridged; a full example is in input_samples/01_generate_schedule.request.json):

{
  "courses": [
    {
      "course_id": "CPSC-350-01",
      "title": "Data Structures and Algorithms",
      "instructor_id": "FAC-014",
      "expected_enrollment": 34,
      "required_features": ["computer"],
      "preferred_times": ["MW-1030", "TR-1000"],
      "avoid_times": ["MW-0800"],
      "conflicts_with": ["CPSC-380-01"]
    }
  ],
  "timeslots": [
    { "timeslot_id": "MW-1030", "days": ["M", "W"], "start": "10:30", "end": "11:45" }
  ],
  "rooms": [
    { "room_id": "SWE-150", "capacity": 40, "features": ["computer"],
      "building": "SWE", "shared": true, "unavailable_times": [] }
  ],
  "faculty_constraints": [
    { "instructor_id": "FAC-014", "unavailable_times": ["TR-1300"], "preferred_times": ["MW-1030"] }
  ],
  "global_constraints": [],
  "context": { "unit_code": "SENG" }
}

Required result:

{
  "schedule": [
    {
      "course_id": "CPSC-350-01",
      "timeslot_id": "MW-1030",
      "room_id": "SWE-150",
      "rationale": [
        "Room capacity satisfies expected enrollment.",
        "Room provides required computing feature.",
        "Time is preferred by the instructor."
      ],
      "preference_score": 0.91
    }
  ],
  "unresolved": [],
  "summary": "All courses scheduled with no hard-constraint violations."
}

preference_score must be a number from 0.0 to 1.0 representing how strongly the agent prefers the selected assignment relative to other feasible choices. You decide how to compute or estimate this score, but document your method.

Unresolved items may be course-ID strings or objects of the form {"course_id": ..., "reason": ...}. Objects are preferred because they explain why. Every course in the request must appear either in schedule or in unresolved. The provost counts a course that appears in neither as silently dropped.

Operation 2: evaluate_change Updated

The coordinator uses this operation when it needs your unit to consider moving one course because of a university-wide conflict. The proposal is always free of conflicts with the other units. Your job is to judge it against your own unit's constraints and preferences.

{
  "current_schedule": [ ... ],
  "proposed_change": {
    "course_id": "CPSC-350-01",
    "new_timeslot_id": "TR-1000",
    "new_room_id": "SWE-150"
  },
  "reason": "Resolve shared room SWE-150: SBE ECON-201-02 holds MW-1030/SWE-150.",
  "courses": [ ... ], "timeslots": [ ... ], "rooms": [ ... ],
  "faculty_constraints": [ ... ], "global_constraints": [ ... ], "context": { ... }
}

The reason names the conflict type and the other unit's course that keeps the contested resource. The conflict types are shared room, shared instructor and cross list mismatch.

Required result:

{
  "decision": "reject",
  "reason": "The proposed time conflicts with another required course.",
  "impact_score": 0.82,
  "alternatives": [
    {
      "timeslot_id": "MW-1300",
      "room_id": "SWE-150",
      "impact_score": 0.22,
      "reason": "No hard conflicts and only a minor preference penalty."
    }
  ]
}
  • decision must be accept or reject.
  • impact_score must be between 0.0 and 1.0, where 0.0 means essentially no disruption and 1.0 means severe disruption or infeasibility.
  • If you reject a proposal and at least one valid alternative exists, return one or more alternatives.
  • A rejection must identify the specific constraint or preference that caused the rejection.
  • An alternative must move the course. Offering the course's current placement, which is the one in conflict, is not an alternative. Alternatives that reuse the contested resource at an overlapping time can't be used by the provost.
  • Accept is not a commit. The provost decides and commits changes. You see the outcome in the next request's current_schedule.

Operation 3: repair_schedule Updated

The coordinator uses this operation after a disruption, such as a room becoming unavailable, a faculty constraint changing, or enrollment increasing.

{
  "current_schedule": [ ... ],
  "disruptions": [
    { "type": "room_unavailable", "room_id": "SWE-150" }
  ],
  "timeslots": [ ... ],
  "rooms": [ ... ],
  "courses": [ ... ],
  "faculty_constraints": [ ... ],
  "global_constraints": [ ... ],
  "context": { ... }
}

The envelope in the request already reflects the disruption. For example, a closed room has been removed from rooms. The disruptions list tells you why things changed. Disruption types:

typefieldseffect on the envelope
room_unavailableroom_id, optional timeslot_idsWithout timeslot_ids the room is removed. With them, they are added to the room's unavailable_times.
room_capacity_changeroom_id, new_capacityThe room's capacity is updated.
faculty_unavailableinstructor_id, timeslot_idsThe slots are added to the instructor's unavailable_times.
enrollment_changecourse_id, new_enrollment, optional unitThe course's expected_enrollment is updated.
blackoutdays, start, endA blackout entry is added to global_constraints.
course_must_movecourse_id, forbidden_timeslot_idsA forbidden_timeslots entry is added to global_constraints.

Required result:

{
  "schedule": [ ... ],
  "changes": [
    {
      "course_id": "CPSC-350-01",
      "old_timeslot_id": "MW-1030",
      "old_room_id": "SWE-150",
      "new_timeslot_id": "MW-1030",
      "new_room_id": "SWE-201",
      "reason": "Original room unavailable."
    }
  ],
  "change_count": 1,
  "unresolved": [],
  "summary": "Schedule repaired with one course moved."
}

The repair objective is not simply to produce any new schedule. Your agent should preserve as much of the existing schedule as possible while restoring feasibility. change_count must equal the length of changes. The provost measures stability by comparing your returned schedule with current_schedule, not by trusting change_count.

Escalations. When a university-wide conflict can't be resolved by moving a single course, the provost sends an ordinary repair_schedule carrying a reservation: a room_unavailable or faculty_unavailable with specific timeslot_ids, or a course_must_move that forbids every slot but one. Your repair may need to move other courses to satisfy it.

Protocol Details New

Additional envelope fields

FieldMeaning
rooms[].unavailable_timesHard. The room can't be used at any slot that overlaps one of these slots.
rooms[].sharedOther units also hold this room, so it may be contested.
rooms[].buildingInformational.
faculty_constraints[].unavailable_timesHard. Overlap semantics apply.
contextUnit code and data-provenance notes. Informational.

global_constraints types

{"type": "blackout", "days": ["W"], "start": "12:00", "end": "13:00"}
{"type": "forbidden_timeslots", "course_id": "CPSC-350-01", "timeslot_ids": ["MW-1030"]}
{"type": "cross_list_match", "rule": "same_timeslot", "owner": "<unit name>",
 "courses": [{"unit": "<unit A>", "course_id": "..."}, {"unit": "<unit B>", "course_id": "..."}]}
  • blackout: Hard. No course may meet in any slot that overlaps the window.
  • forbidden_timeslots: Hard. The named course may not use the listed slots.
  • cross_list_match: The two sections are one combined class taught across units, and they must end up in the same timeslot. You can't see the other unit's placement during generate_schedule. Placing the course at its preferred time is a good guess. The provost aligns the pair afterwards: it asks the non-owner first, through evaluate_change or an escalation.

Error Handling Requirements

  • Never invent a room or time slot that is absent from the coordinator input.
  • Never silently ignore an unschedulable course.
  • If a complete valid schedule is impossible, return status = partial and list the unscheduled courses in unresolved.
  • If the request is malformed or your agent cannot operate, return status = failed and explain the error.
  • Do not crash the integration process because one course cannot be scheduled.

The Provost Orchestrator New

The provost is the coordinator your agent works with, both locally and on integration day. Every run follows the same phases:

PhaseWhat happens
1 · GenerateAll 11 units receive generate_schedule in parallel.
2 · ValidateEvery returned schedule is checked against the unit's envelope and the interface schema. Violating entries are dropped and become unresolved.
3 · NegotiateThe provost merges the schedules and finds conflicts between units: shared rooms, shared instructors and cross-list mismatches. For each conflict it proposes a conflict-free move to one side through evaluate_change. It asks the unit that has conceded least so far first. On rejection it tries your alternatives, then the other unit. If no single move works, it escalates with repair_schedule.
4 · DisruptScripted disruptions are applied. Each affected unit receives repair_schedule, and then negotiation runs again.
5 · ReportThe final university schedule, per-unit metrics and the full message log are written to outputs/<run-id>/.

The provost holds the authoritative university schedule. Units propose; the provost commits. After your unit concedes a resource, the provost records the other unit's claim in your envelope as unavailable_times, so your later repairs don't move back into it.

Connecting your agent

Your agent does not run a server. The connector dials out to the provost, waits for requests, calls your run_agent, and sends back what it returns. Only outbound network access is needed.

# practice: provost running in Docker on your own laptop
python student_kit/connect.py --unit SENG --agent path/to/agent.py --team "Team Name"

# final integration: the instructor's provost on the class network
python student_kit/connect.py --unit SENG --agent path/to/agent.py --team "Team Name" \
    --provost http://<instructor-ip>:8370

Practice environment

  1. Install Docker Desktop and Python 3.9 or newer, then clone the course repository.
  2. Start the provost with docker compose up (it pulls a prebuilt image) and open http://localhost:8370. That page is the live dashboard.
  3. Connect your agent with the command above, then click Start run. A reference agent plays the other 10 units, and the practice data is the same shape as the final data.
  4. Read your results on the dashboard and in outputs/<run-id>/. That folder contains final_schedule.csv, metrics.json, moves.json, and messages.jsonl (every request and response).

Final Integration Exercise Updated

On final integration day, every team connects to the instructor's provost on the class network (address announced in class). The coordinator will provide each unit with its allocated rooms and time slots and request a schedule. The coordinator will then identify university-wide issues and ask individual academic-unit agents to evaluate changes. After the initial schedule converges, one or more disruptions will be introduced.

Possible disruptions include:

  • A classroom becomes unavailable.
  • A room's capacity changes.
  • A faculty member becomes unavailable during a previously valid time.
  • Enrollment in a course increases.
  • A new university-wide blackout period is introduced.
  • A course must be moved with minimal disruption to the rest of the schedule.

The final envelopes use the full section list for each unit, which is larger than the practice subset. Make sure your agent finishes within the timeouts at that scale. Keep your connector running until the instructor ends the run.

Your grade does not depend on the entire class-wide system producing a perfect university schedule. You are graded primarily on the quality, reliability, interface compliance, and evaluation of your academic-unit agent.

Important Design Principles

  • A more complex agent is not automatically a better agent.
  • Hard constraints must be validated, not merely described in a prompt.
  • Historical schedules are evidence of prior practice, not authoritative requirements.
  • A scheduling agent should be able to explain why it made important choices.
  • When a request cannot be satisfied, a useful agent explains the problem and offers alternatives.
  • The final integrated system is multi-agent because scheduling knowledge and decision authority are distributed across academic units.
